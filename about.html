About the calculator

This tool estimates the Doppler pitch and tempo shift heard by an observer as a performer marches in a straight line. Enter the performer’s start and end dots (yard line, side, steps inside/outside and in front of/behind a marker, plus height), the observer position, the environmental conditions used to compute the speed of sound, and the music parameters (tempo, counts, and source frequency or note).

Defaults place the observer in the Lucas Oil Stadium judges box and use environmental conditions estimated for the stadium interior. Press Calculate to update the field plot, the tuning- and tempo-shift chart, and the numerical results. Under Listen, play a sine tone of the performer’s frequency, the time-varying frequency heard by the observer, or both combined for the duration of the move.

Scale of the effect

The change in pitch due to Doppler shifts may seem trivial, but the effect is actually quite large relative to what would normally be accepted as playing "in tune" while standing still. At typical marching speeds, the pitch heard by the observer is about 10 cents different from what is played by the performer. A performer marching straight at an observer at 180bpm at a 6-to-5 step size playing A4=440Hz will be heard by the observer as A4=442Hz. The effect is directional as well. So if one set of performers is marching towards the front sideline while another set is marching away from the front sideline, they can easily be 20 cents apart as heard by the observer. This effect also means that individual performers who are mic’d while on the move will automatically be out of tune with themselves. This is because the mic’d sound coming from the speakers is not Doppler shifted while the sound coming from the bell of the instrument is Doppler shifted.

Bio

Jake Diamond marched with the Bluecoats Drum and Bugle Corps in 2017 and 2018. With the corps, he traveled to Hong Kong to perform in the 2019 Chinese New Year Parade, later returning to march with the organization’s alumni corps in 2022. He began his drum corps career with the Blue Stars in 2016. He also spent three years with the Marching Illini at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, serving as section leader for two of those years. During his time with the Marching Illini, he performed in the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade and the Dublin St. Patrick’s Day Parade, and appeared in the film Captive State.

Jake is currently the Robert J. Eichelberger Distinguished Postdoctoral Fellow at the Army Research Laboratory in Aberdeen, Maryland. His research examines the mechanics of materials subjected to shock, impact, and high-rate loading. He earned his Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Johns Hopkins University in 2025 and holds an M.S. and B.S. in Materials Science and Engineering from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, graduating with Highest Honors.
