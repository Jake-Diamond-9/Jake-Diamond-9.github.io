About the calculator

This tool estimates the Doppler pitch and tempo shift heard by an observer as a performer marches in a straight line across a football field. Enter the performer’s start and end dots (yard line, side, steps inside/outside and in front of/behind a marker, plus height), the observer position, the environmental conditions used to compute the speed of sound, and the music parameters (tempo, counts, and source frequency or note).

Defaults place the observer in the Lucas Oil Stadium judges box and use environmental conditions estimated for the stadium interior. Press Calculate to update the field plot, the tuning- and tempo-shift chart, and the numerical results. Under Listen, play a sine tone of the performer’s frequency, the time-varying frequency heard by the observer, or both combined for the duration of the move.

Scale of the effect

For typical marching moves, the Doppler effect changes the heard tuning by about 10 cents. That amount is generally considered out of tune when the source is stationary. Musicians also regularly make tuning adjustments of about 10 cents when moving from equal temperament toward just intonation.

About Jake Diamond

Jake Diamond spent three years with the Marching Illini at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where he served as baritone section leader for two of those years. During this time, he performed in the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, the Dublin St. Patrick’s Day Parade, and appeared in the film Captive State. His involvement in the drum corps activity began with the Blue Stars in 2016, followed by two seasons with the Bluecoats in 2017 and 2018. With the Bluecoats, he traveled to Hong Kong to perform in the 2019 Chinese New Year Parade and later returned to march with the organization’s alumni corps in 2022.

Jake is currently the Robert J. Eichelberger Distinguished Postdoctoral Fellow at the Army Research Laboratory in Aberdeen, Maryland. His research examines the mechanics of materials subjected to shock, impact, and high-rate loading. He earned his Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Johns Hopkins University in 2025 and holds an M.S. and B.S. in Materials Science and Engineering from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, graduating with Highest Honors.
